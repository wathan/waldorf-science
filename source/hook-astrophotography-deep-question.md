# Student-Facing Hook — Hubble/JWST Astrophotography for the Deep Question

*Micro-level teaching input (pilot-teacher lesson planning), not decided meso language.* **Provisional working draft** — a concrete opening move the pilot teachers may adopt, adapt, or drop; nothing here is ratified block content. Anchors to the Session-1 deep question and the [Session-2 phase structure](session2-phase-structure-draft.md). Companion: [Meso-Design Plan](meso-design-plan-cosmology-optics-pilot.md).

---

## What this hook is for

- **(B) Deep question** — *"How do we know what's out there — what can light tell us, and where does seeing end and inferring begin?"*
- **Entry point.** Students arrive already loving space images. The hook uses that appetite — but turns the image from decoration into the crime scene.
- **Core move.** A Hubble/JWST image *looks like seeing* and *is mostly inferring*. That collision **is** the deep question, handed to the class on day one. The awe is the bait; the rug-pull is the lesson.

## Placement in the phase arc

The hook front-loads the whole arc, then gets paid off twice:

| Where | Use |
|---|---|
| **Phase 1 opening (wk 1)** | Run the full sequence below as the block's first 15 min. Plant the question *before* any optics content — the image is a phenomenon, met before concept (Waldorf phenomenology; consistent with Phase 1's "what is directly perceived, before any instrument"). Leave it **unresolved**. |
| **Phase 2 pivot (wk 3)** | Return to the same image once spectroscopy/redshift have made light-as-evidence explicit. Now the class can *name* what the telescope actually recorded (photon counts per filter) and where the human choices entered. The rug-pull becomes mechanism, not just provocation. |
| **Phase 4 capstone (wk 6)** | The image is available as one candidate "graded phenomenon" — a boundary-drawing artifact (satisfies the Tier-1 *synthetic, not categorical* criterion; de-personalised, so class-biography-safe). Task built at micro. |

## The epistemology payload — why the pretty picture counts as *inferring*

Three facts do the work. Each converts awe into the seeing/inferring boundary.

1. **False colour / the "Hubble palette" (SHO).** Nebula colours are not colours. Sulfur → red, hydrogen → green, oxygen → blue. The image is an **element map wearing a photograph's clothes**. A person chose that mapping.
2. **JWST is infrared.** That light is invisible to any eye, by definition. Every JWST image is *already* a translation of light no human receives into light one can. The inference is baked in before the first pixel is seen.
3. **The sensor recorded numbers, not a picture.** What came down is photon counts per filter — a grid of numbers. The glossy image is a human *reconstruction* of that grid. Show the raw grayscale filter frames next to the final composite.

## The single best artifact — Pillars of Creation

Three images, one object:

- **Hubble 1995** (visible)
- **JWST NIRCam 2022** (near-infrared)
- **JWST MIRI 2022** (mid-infrared — looks like a different universe)

Ask: **which one is real?** The class is forced to *none / all / wrong question*. That is the epistemological capstone compressed onto one slide — and it recurs cleanly at every phase.

## The opening sequence (~15 min, Phase 1 day 1)

1. **Full-screen the image. Silence.** Let awe do its work. No commentary.
2. **"Could your eye see this from a spaceship window?"** — No. Let that land.
3. **Reveal the raw filter frames + the colour choice.** *"A scientist decided the red."*
4. **Pillars triptych.** Which is true?
5. **Land the question — theirs now, not the poster's:** *"So — what can light actually tell us? And where, in this picture, did seeing stop and inferring start?"*

Write the deep question on the board here. Do not resolve it. It stays open until Phase 2 gives the class tools to reopen it with evidence.

## Runner-up images (backup / rotation)

- **Hubble Deep Field.** Point at "empty" black sky, then reveal ~10,000 galaxies. *"You saw nothing. Nothing was wrong."* Clean seeing-vs-what's-there.
- **Gravitational lensing arcs (SMACS 0723, JWST first deep field).** The smeared arcs are galaxies bent by mass you cannot see; the mass is *inferred from* the distortion. Pure "seeing end / inferring begin," and previews Phase 3's inference chain.

## Caution — the failure mode

Awe left alone goes passive: *"science = magic pictures."* The rug-pull is what converts awe into the epistemic question. **Do not skip step 3.** Beauty without the disclosure teaches the opposite of the deep question — that the image simply *is* what's out there.
